## 01 — How to Spot a Phishing Email
- **Urgency or threats** — "Your account will be closed in 24 hours!"
- **Sender doesn't match** — display name says "Microsoft", address is `support@micros0ft-help.com`
- **Links go somewhere else** — hover (don't click) to see the real address
- **Unexpected attachments** — especially `.zip`, `.html`, or files asking you to "enable content"
- **Requests for passwords, payment, or gift cards**
- ⚠️ **Perfect spelling doesn't mean safe** — attackers now use AI to write convincing emails. Judge the *request*, not the grammar.

**Unsure?** Don't reply or click — use the **Report** button, or contact IT.

## 02 — Passwords (Plain English)
- Use **three random words** (NCSC advice) — e.g. a phrase made of three unrelated words you can remember. Don't use the example from any guide, including this one.
- Longer beats complicated: length matters more than symbols
- **Never reuse** a password — especially your work or email password
- Use a **password manager** (e.g. Bitwarden, or your browser's built-in one)
- Turn on **2-step verification (MFA)** everywhere it's offered
- **Never approve an MFA prompt you didn't start** — that's an attacker with your password

## 03 — "I Think I've Been Hacked" — What to Do NOW
1. **Don't turn the computer off** — it destroys evidence
2. **Disconnect from the network** — turn off Wi-Fi or unplug the cable
3. **Call IT/security immediately** — use the phone, not email from the affected device
4. **Don't try to fix it yourself** or delete anything
5. **Write down** what you saw, what you clicked, and when

No one gets in trouble for reporting quickly — delays cause the damage.

## 04 — Social Engineering
| Trick | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Pretexting** | Caller claims to be IT and asks for your password or MFA code |
| **Baiting** | "Found" USB stick in the car park |
| **Tailgating** | Someone follows you through a secure door without badging |
| **Smishing / vishing** | Scam texts or calls, often "from the bank" or "a parcel company" |

**Rule:** if something feels off, **verify through a separate, known channel** before acting.
